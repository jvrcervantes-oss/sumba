Sumba is one of Indonesia's last genuinely wild islands. No Grab, no tourist shuttle buses, no organised day trips in air-conditioned vans. If you want to see the real island — the megalithic clan tombs, the deserted surf beaches, the savannah roads with nobody on them — you need your own transport. A motorcycle is the best way to do it.

This guide covers everything: which bike to rent, what to expect on Sumba's roads, what to pack, how pricing works, and how to book with airport delivery so your adventure starts the second you land.

Why Rent a Motorcycle in Sumba?

The distances in Sumba are larger than they look on a map. From Lede Kalumbang Airport to Weekuri Lake is 45 km. From Waikabubak to Nihiwatu Beach is 60 km. There is no public transport that covers these routes on a useful schedule. Taxis exist but they're expensive for multi-day use and won't wait while you spend two hours at a waterfall.

A motorcycle gives you complete freedom — leave when you want, stop where you want, take the side road when it looks interesting. Sumba rewards exactly this kind of exploration. The most memorable spots are not on any organised tour itinerary.

Sumba's Roads — What to Expect

West Sumba has a mix of road conditions. The main road from Tambolaka to Waikabubak is tarmac and in reasonable shape. Secondary roads to coastal destinations vary — some are well-paved, others are compacted dirt that turns into clay mud during rain. There are also sections of loose volcanic rock on routes leading to waterfalls and remote beaches.

None of this is technical riding — you don't need any off-road experience. But you do need a bike that won't fall apart when the road turns to dirt. A standard rental scooter would struggle with Sumba's secondary roads. The BH-G3 handles them comfortably.

Road conditions by area

  • Tambolaka → Waikabubak: Tarmac, good condition, 42 km
  • Waikabubak → Weekuri Lake: Mixed tarmac and gravel, 40 km
  • Waikabubak → Nihiwatu area: Mostly tarmac with short dirt sections, 60 km
  • Wanokaka coast roads: Dirt and loose stone, ride carefully in rain
  • Anakalang villages: Paved access roads, some village paths on dirt

Best Time to Ride in Sumba

The dry season in West Sumba runs roughly April to November. This is the best time to ride — roads are dry, the savannah turns golden, and coastal routes are accessible.

The wet season (December to March) brings heavy rain, particularly in January and February. Dirt roads can become impassable after heavy overnight rain. Riding is still possible but you need to check conditions daily. The landscape is spectacularly green in the wet season if you don't mind the mud.

What to Pack for Riding in Sumba

  • Rain gear (lightweight jacket) — even in dry season, afternoon showers happen
  • Sunscreen — the Sumba sun is intense; reapply every few hours
  • Offline maps — download the area on Maps.me or Google Maps before you go; mobile data is patchy outside towns
  • Cash (IDR) — fuel stations, warungs and guesthouses outside main towns don't take cards
  • A tube repair kit — sold at any hardware store for <20k IDR, peace of mind on remote roads
  • Water bottle — at least 1.5L, hydration is serious in Sumba's heat
  • A bungee cord — for securing bags to the rear rack

Riding Rules and Licence Requirements

Technically, you need a valid motorcycle licence (A or A1) and an International Driving Permit (IDP) to legally ride in Indonesia. In practice, tourist checks in Sumba are rare compared to Bali. However, the IDP is worth getting before your trip — it's cheap, takes 20 minutes at most national automobile clubs, and removes the hassle if you're stopped.

Helmets are compulsory by law. We provide two full-face helmets with every rental — there's no reason to ride without one.
